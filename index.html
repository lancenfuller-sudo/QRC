The water metaphor

Formless until poured.

Water has no fixed shape. Capital doesn’t either — until you choose the vessel. Discipline is knowing which vessel you are filling, and why.

Cup

Spending — near-term outflow you can see and empty with intention.

Bottle

Reserves — sealed enough to last, open enough to reach when needed.

Teapot

Plan for the next generation — pour carefully; leave something warm behind.

Ocean

Markets move. Water can compound — or crash. Readiness is the practice.

“Be water” here means adaptability — a widely known martial-arts teaching. Flow Fund is not affiliated with the Bruce Lee Estate.

Therefore we use three plain examples of flow. Which one fits depends on the person — and what already feels true in their life.

Martial arts

Stay soft enough to change shape, firm enough to hold a line. Do not meet force with rigidity — a rigid stance snaps when the current shifts.

Surfing

Read the set, paddle into position, and commit when the wave is real. Fighting every ripple is thrash; timing with the ocean is flow.

Golf

Choose the club, trust the line, stay soft through the swing. Gripping too hard costs distance; quiet commitment is flow.

Household capital asks for the same practice: shape to the conditions, keep a sound vessel, and move when the moment matches the purpose.
